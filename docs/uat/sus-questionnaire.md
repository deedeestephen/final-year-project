# System Usability Scale (SUS)

The System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996) is ten statements answered from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). It is free to use; cite Brooke, J. (1996) "SUS: a 'quick and dirty' usability scale", in *Usability Evaluation in Industry*, Taylor & Francis.

**Giving it:** after the tasks and before the interview. Ask the participant to answer quickly, with their first impression, and to answer every statement (if unsure, the middle, 3). For participants who prefer it, **read each statement aloud** and point to the five boxes, or show five faces from very unhappy to very happy; write down the number they choose. Say "the system" means the app (or the website) they just used. Do not change the wording; translations must be checked by a qualified translator before use.

## Scoring

1. For statements **1, 3, 5, 7 and 9** (the positive ones): score = answer − 1.
2. For statements **2, 4, 6, 8 and 10** (the negative ones): score = 5 − answer.
3. Add the ten scores (0 to 40) and **multiply by 2.5**. The result is between 0 and 100. It is not a percentage.

**Example:** answers 4, 2, 4, 1, 4, 2, 5, 1, 4, 2 → odd statements 3 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 16; even statements 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 17; (16 + 17) × 2.5 = **82.5**.

**Reading the score:** the average across many published studies is about 68. The proposal's target is **at least 75** (NFR-04). Report the mean, standard deviation and range per user group and overall, and say how many people answered each group. A form with an unanswered statement is scored with 3 for that statement and the fact is noted.
